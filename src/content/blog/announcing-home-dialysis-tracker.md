Adapdev has been a one-product shop since launch. Today we're saying
publicly what we've been scoping privately: there is a second product in
development, and it's called the **Home Dialysis Tracker**.

## What it is

It's a logbook for people doing their own haemodialysis at home. Every
session the patient runs produces a short list of facts the nephrology
team wants to see — pre- and post-session blood pressure, weight, venous
and arterial pressures off the machine, conductivity, UF volume and
rate, any alarms, how the patient felt afterwards. Today, most of that
is either paper, a photo of paper, or lost in the gap between the
patient's phone and the clinic's EMR. The Tracker's job is to make that
record easy to keep and easy to share — in that order.

## What it is not

It is not a replacement for a nephrology EMR. It does not control a
dialysis machine, and it does not treat anyone. It's a patient-owned
record with a clean export the renal unit can read.

## Why Adapdev

The shape of this problem is the shape Adapdev likes. The data belongs
to the patient; the software has to work when the internet doesn't; the
dialect the app uses has to match the dialect the unit uses, not a
one-size-fits-all "wellness record". That is the same model as ADMS
(client, matter, job) applied to a very different domain (patient,
session, run).

## Where it's up to

It's in active development. We're writing the data model with
nephrologists and home-dialysis nurses right now, so that when we ship
beta the vocabulary is already theirs. Early access is forming for
**2027**; pricing will be published closer to beta.

If you're a home-dialysis patient, carer, nephrologist, or home-therapy
nurse and this sounds interesting, email
