The default assumption in software, for about the last ten years, has been
that nothing serious should run on your own hardware any more. Email went
to the cloud. Calendars went to the cloud. Documents, invoicing,
accounting, CRM, HR, project management — all gone to someone else's
infrastructure, billed per seat per month in US dollars.

That default has not worked out as well as the slide decks suggested, and
for the particular segment Adapdev builds for — sole traders, small
businesses, small-to-medium law firms — it is often the wrong answer.

## What on-premises actually buys you

> **The things cloud doesn't do.**
> Not "cloud bad" — cloud is excellent at things on-premises can't do.
> But the reverse list is longer than most people realise.

A small list of what you get when the software lives on hardware you own:

1. **No monthly bill in US dollars.** A $12/seat SaaS tool, times four
   people, times twelve months, is $576 a year, every year, forever. A
   one-off licence paid once is cheaper by year two.
2. **Your data stays yours.** If Adapdev disappears tomorrow, your ADMS
   database is still on your server. You still have your clients, your
   matters, your audit log. The only thing you lose is future updates.
3. **It works when the internet doesn't.** Rural Australian internet is
   improving but still uneven. A sole-trader electrician in Longreach
   should not have software that stops working because the NBN did.
4. **No forced upgrades the morning before the client meeting.** You pick
   when a new version lands, not the vendor.
5. **No vendor-side data breach.** When a SaaS has an incident, every
   customer's data is in the same bucket. When an on-premises install
   has an incident, it is one site, one tenant, one blast radius.

## What on-premises doesn't do well

To be honest about the trade: on-premises will not give you a
collaborative Google-Doc experience, zero-config multi-device sync, or
"log in from your phone at the airport". If you need those things, use
software that does them well.

But for the specific question of *where this client's matter file lives*,
on-premises is still the right default. ADMS exists to make that default
easy.
